Rules:
- "keep" = a real order/purchase confirmation, shipping notice, receipt, invoice, account security matter, legal/personal correspondence, or anything with a specific reference number, dollar amount, or dated appointment.
- "trash" = generic marketing: sales, percent-off offers, newsletters, cart-abandonment nudges, review-request nags.
- "mixed" = genuinely unsure from subject alone.
- category/retention/expense_type/folder_domain/needs_attention only matter when action is "keep" or "mixed" — for "trash" set category to "marketing", retention to "transient", expense_type to "n/a", folder_domain to "Other", needs_attention to false.

Retention guidance — forever is narrow and deliberate, not a default:
- transient (~1 week): expires fast — OTP/verification codes, and anything that's just a duplicate of an alert the platform itself keeps.
- standard (~3 years, the default for ordinary keeps): routine receipts, shipping confirmations, account statements, renewals.
- forever (use sparingly): legal documents, contracts, personal correspondence, or a receipt for a durable good you're likely to own for years.
- pending-review: genuinely ambiguous — leave it here rather than guessing.

Vendor-specific overrides go here as you find them. Example pattern: a
marketplace whose own order history is itself a durable record doesn't need
its routine receipts kept too — trash the small/consumable orders, keep only
large-ticket or long-life items, and default to trash rather than keep when
it's ambiguous (the marketplace's own history is the fallback either way).

expense_type guidance: list your own business senders here as you identify
them; everything else is personal; anything that isn't a financial item at
all (a security alert, a personal note) is "n/a".

needs_attention = true only for a pending action (unpaid balance, expiring
card, required reply) — not for routine kept mail.
